# Product Design-Support Checklist

Design owns the solution and the final UX call. Product's job is to make sure design is solving the right problem, with the right evidence, inside clear limits, and to catch weak decisions before they reach the user.

## Working with the designer partner, from day one

Guesses are messes. Every gap product leaves in a conversation, design fills with a guess, so communication is direct, written down and early.

| What product does | Failure it prevents |
|---|---|
| Bring the designer in from the start: discovery, client meetings and problem framing, not only once the brief is written | Design receiving a finished brief with no chance to question the problem |
| Say what's critical and what can wait, and name the trade-offs when two needs compete | Design spending effort on the wrong thing, or guessing at priorities |
| Give feedback as a fresh pair of eyes, as a partner, not as a judge or a competitor | Critique that feels like a contest, so issues get defended instead of fixed |


## Discover

| What product does | Failure it prevents |
|---|---|
| Hand design a sharp problem, so research looks in the right place | Building the right solution to the wrong problem |
| Match the research method to the question: interviews for why, data for how many | Treating a few loud quotes as a trend, or a number as a reason |
| Plan neutral interview questions that cover every side of the problem | Research that confirms what the team believed, or gaps found only at synthesis |
| Audit AI synthesis against the transcripts before sharing it | Overstated themes becoming "facts" in the brief |
| Name the least-understood role and the unproven business assumptions | Design optimizing for the easy user and missing the risky one |

## Define

| What product does | Failure it prevents |
|---|---|
| Rewrite solution-shaped requests as problems before they reach design | A brief that closes the design space before design starts |
| Rank problem statements by depth of evidence, not by how often they came up | Prioritizing what's loud over what's real |
| State goals, definitions and constraints up front | Design guessing at business rules |

## Architect

| What product does | Failure it prevents |
|---|---|
| Agree the journey and the goal of each phase before anyone draws screens | Prescribing screens before the structure is agreed |
| Stress-test every flow for the different states: success, empty, loading, error, partial, cancelled and permission states | A flow that only works on the happy path |


## Design

| What product does | Failure it prevents |
|---|---|
| Supply real edge-case content, not layout opinions | Designs that only look right with perfect data |
| Set user and business objectives before giving feedback | Critique that turns into personal preference |
| Do an own critique pass before asking AI, then compare | AI output anchoring what product sees |
| Phrase feedback as goal-framed questions and flag taste as taste | Pixel feedback that design can't act on |


## Validate and hand off

| What product does | Failure it prevents |
|---|---|
| Write a design-ready brief: who has the problem, what the problem is, why it matters, the evidence, the goal and the constraints | A brief that jumps to a solution or leaves design to guess the basics |
| Start from the problem statement, and leave the solution to design | A brief that closes the design space before design starts |
| Run the handoff checklist before work changes hands | Gaps discovered during build |

## Across every phase: working with AI

| What product does | Failure it prevents |
|---|---|
| Treat AI output as a draft: judge it against the user, the brief and the evidence | Shipping the first output |
| Ask for the evidence behind each claim and check it yourself | Confident claims with nothing behind them |
| Push back and iterate in conversation | Accepting a first answer that's close but wrong |
| Don't skip the designer by generating finished UI | Design reduced to decorating product's screens |
