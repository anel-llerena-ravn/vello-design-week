**How to read this document**

- **Said** means a participant stated it. Quotes are verbatim from the transcripts.
- **Inferred** means my interpretation. It is not a finding until tested.
- Counts are participants out of 6. P03-D's statements count under the P03 session and are labelled separately.
- **Depth** rates the evidence behind a theme:

| Depth | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Deep | Participants describe things that happened to them |
| Medium | A mix of experience and stated opinion |
| Shallow | Opinion, secondhand accounts, or talk about a future need |

- The sample is small (3 requesters, 2 providers, 1 admin). A count of 2 of 2 providers is not the same strength as 5 of 6 overall.

## 1. Trust comes from a named person who vouches

**Count:** 5 of 6 (P01, P02, P04, P05, P06). **Depth:** Deep. Everyone except P03 describes a real referral they gave or received.

**Said**

- P01: "if you gave me the choice between a man with a certificate who I've never seen before and a man with no certificate who Priya has used for two years, I'm taking Priya's man."
- P02: "It's a person taking responsibility, that's not the same as a company saying trust us."
- Also: P04 got all 14 regular clients by referral and has never advertised. P06's only rule for the list is that "Somebody who lives here has to have used them and said they were good." P05: "They don't find me, they know me."

**Scope of the claim**

- 5 participants rely on referral.
- Only 2 (P01, P05) directly say referral beats formal checks. P02 is split: they rely on referral for Marta but ask for checks for childcare. The first draft titled this theme "outweighs platform checks," which overstated a comparison most participants didn't make.

**Contradicting evidence**

- P02 wants formal checks for childcare. See the loud-but-shallow flag.
- P01 booked a sitter nobody had vouched for when stuck: "I booked someone off an app who I had never met, based on reviews, and she was great."

**Inferred**

- Referral and checks may answer different questions. Referral: *will this person treat my home well and turn up?* Checks: *is this person dangerous?* Only P02 and P01's sitter story speak to the second question, so the link to childcare is a hypothesis, not a pattern.
- Need can override a stated rule (P01's August sitter). What people say they'd never do is a weak predictor of what they do.

## 2. The referral network is hard to get into

**Count:** 4 of 6 (P02, P03, P04, P06). **Depth:** Deep.

**Said**

- P02: "Everyone tells you word of mouth, word of mouth, and it's like, yes, but I need a mouth."
- P06: "So the list is a bit of a closed shop and I'm aware of it."
- Also: P03 on how many neighbours she could ask: "None, I should think. I wouldn't knock on a door." P04: "If I want two new clients this month, I can't make that happen." P06's ironing provider was listed "about four months after she should have" been because P06 didn't recognise the voucher.

**Scope of the claim**

- The first draft counted P05 and titled the theme "locked out." P05 is inside the network and speaks only about his younger self ("if you asked me at thirty"), so he's no longer counted.
- P04 isn't locked out (14 clients). Growth is slow and out of P04's control. "Hard to get into" fits better than "locked out."

**Contradicting evidence**

- P01 got a usable recommendation within about a week from a 90-person WhatsApp group.
- P02 found Denise within eight months, calling it luck: "I got lucky because she's the sort of person who knocks on your door with a cake."
- P03 has other channels: her daughter and the church list ("That's how I got the gardener").
- P06 thinks weaker signals could add up: "Three unknowns might be worth one Denise."

**Inferred**

- The network works for people already inside it (P01, P05) and fails at the edges: newcomers, isolated older residents, and providers building a client base.

## 3. Reliability is the quality most often named

**Count:** 4 of 6 (P01, P03, P04, P05). **Depth:** Deep for requesters, self-reported for providers.

**Said**

- P01, on what they want to know: "Whether he'll turn up." and "That's what ends the relationship. Not anything dramatic."
- P05: "Turning up. That's ninety per cent of it."
- Also: P01's previous cleaner "cancelled the morning of, three times." P04 says clients "care that I come on the day I said and that I'm the same person every week." P03 lost a morning to a two-hour arrival window: "Just tell me a time. Not a window, a time."

**Scope of the claim**

- The first draft titled this "Showing up matters more than safety credentials." Only P01 made that comparison.
- P04 and P05 are describing their own selling point, so their evidence is self-reported.
- P03's point is about exact arrival time, which is related to reliability but different.

**Contradicting evidence**

- P02 never raises reliability. P03-D's main worry is price.

**Inferred**

- The two requesters with first-hand stories (P01's cancellations, P03's lost morning) both describe time lost, not harm.

## 4. Ongoing work is a different job from one-offs

**Count:** 4 of 6 (P01, P02, P04, P05). **Depth:** Deep.

**Said**

- P01: "One-off thing, in and out, I don't care who it is, just be competent. Ongoing thing, someone in my house, someone with my kids, completely different question."
- P04: "If it just sends me one-off strangers across town it's making my week worse, not better."
- Also: P04: "If I'm on time three times, we're fine after that, they stop watching." and "The key is the moment." P01: "Keys. It's the keys." P02: "And Marta's got a key now." P05 says for small jobs "they'll get someone off the internet." For anything people care about, they wait.

**Contradicting evidence**

- P04 takes one-off "big clean" work twice a year, and P01 was satisfied with a one-off TaskRabbit job. One-offs have a place.

**Inferred**

- Handing over a key marks the shift from trial to trust. Three participants brought up keys when talking about trust (P02's came after being asked what they checked).
- Providers judge a platform by whether it produces repeat clients near their existing routes, not by job count.

## 5. "Local" means overlap, not distance

**Count:** 4 of 6 (P01, P02, P04, P06). **Depth:** Medium. P04's work pattern is first-hand. P01's definition is an opinion.

**Said**

- P01: "It's not really distance, it's whether the paths cross."
- P04: "I don't live in Kestrel Park either. I can't afford Kestrel Park."
- Also: P02, on a generic "neighbour used them" label: "that's just a stranger who lives nearby." P06 on the boundary: "That is the great unanswerable question."

**Contradicting evidence**

- P01 values that Tomás lives nearby: "Because he lives here. If he did something wrong, he'd still have to walk past my house every day with somebody else's dog."
- P05's standing depends on having "been inside sixty houses within half a mile."
- Against both: P04's clients have "never asked. Not once, in nine years" where P04 lives.

**Inferred**

- For requesters, social overlap (shared school, shop, streets, people) counts for more than a mapped radius. This rests mostly on P01, backed by P02's reaction to the anonymous "neighbour" label.
- A neighbours-only rule would exclude providers like P04. P04 also claims "Most of the people cleaning those houses live somewhere else." That's one provider's view and hasn't been checked.

## 6. Vouching puts the voucher's reputation at risk

**Count:** 3 of 6 (P02, P05, P06). **Depth:** Medium.

**Said**

- P05: "my name's attached to somebody I don't know, and if they make a mess it comes back on me." and "I'm not lending it to a stranger."
- P06: "the woman came to me, quite angry, as if I'd employed him."
- Also: P02 trusts Denise because "She's not going to put her name on someone who's going to rob me." P06: "If I turn someone down I'd rather they didn't know it was me who did it." P06: "I will not adjudicate a dispute about money between two people I have to see at the shop."

**Contradicting evidence**

- None in the transcripts. No requester was asked what vouching costs the voucher. That's a gap in the questions, not evidence against.

**Inferred**

- Vouching carries weight because it's costly. Making it effortless could weaken the signal.
- The brief expects the admin to moderate disputes, and P06 refuses. That's the sharpest mismatch between the brief and a participant.

## 7. There is no record of who did what or who vouched

**Count:** 3 of 6 (P03, P05, P06). **Depth:** Medium.

**Said**

- P03-D: "She's had four different people in this flat in two years and there's no record of any of it."
- P05, on the tradespeople referral network: "It's just in our heads and on bits of paper."
- Also: P06 wants help with "the chasing, the record of who vouched for whom, the reminder that somebody's been waiting a fortnight." P06: "It's the admin that's eating my evenings."

**Contradicting evidence**

- P06 wants to keep making the decisions: "I'll happily keep making the judgement calls, that's the bit I'm good at."

**Inferred**

- Trust information lives in individual memory (P05, P06) or nowhere (P03). When the holder leaves, it's lost. P03's handyman Ken "retired, or he died" and she had no one after.

## 8. A bad outcome has no route to repair

**Count:** 3 of 6 (P04, P05, P06). **Depth:** Medium. P04 and P06 describe real events. P05's platform scenario is hypothetical.

**Said**

- P04: "There's a button, I pressed it, nothing happened. That review is still there and it's the only one I've got."
- P05: "there's no room to just say sorry and fix it."
- Also: P06, after removing a decorator for one bad job: "I didn't have anything in between. Either you're on it or you're not."

**Contradicting evidence**

- P05's real incident was repaired: he paid for the mirror, did the next job free, and "She still has me now." Repair works face to face. The fear is about platforms.

**Inferred**

- Providers see public reviews as a one-way risk. P05's mirror (repaired in ten minutes, client kept six years) and P06's decorator (removed outright) show what's missing: a step between "fine" and "gone."

## 9. Requesters can't judge a fair price

**Count:** 3 of 6 (P02, P03, P06). **Depth:** Shallow to medium. P02's uncertainty is first-hand. No participant reports an overcharge that actually happened.

**Said**

- P02: "I was never in danger from the boiler man. I just had no way to know if I was being had."
- P03-D: "All the checking in the world doesn't stop someone quoting a stupid number on the doorstep."
- Also: P06, on unconnected older residents: "they're the ones who get charged four hundred pounds for a job worth eighty." (secondhand). P03-D: "She'd pay it and not tell me." (a fear, not an event).

**Related but separate (not counted)**

- P04 wants platform fees clear up front: "A cut of everything feels like it never ends." That's about the platform's cut, not job prices.
- P05 finds quoting hard: "you can't price a wall off three photos."

**Contradicting evidence**

- P01 treats price as secondary: "the price, the schedule, all of that is negotiable, but somebody's got a key."
- P05's point conflicts with P03-D's wish to know "what it costs before he comes." A firm price before a site visit may not be possible for repairs.

**Inferred**

- The first draft said the £400/£80 story came from two independent sessions and "circulates locally." That wasn't supported. P06 hears these stories "from a daughter or a son," so P06 and P03-D may be describing the same kind of case, or even the same case. They can't be treated as independent.

## 10. The person booking isn't always the person served

**Count:** 2 of 6 (P03, P06). **Depth:** Deep but narrow. Only one proxy was interviewed.

**Said**

- P03-D: "every one of these apps assumes the person who needs the thing is the person holding the phone. There's no version where I'm managing it for her."
- P06: "I hear about it afterwards from a daughter or a son."
- Also: the provider "turns up asking for me and Mum has to explain who she is in her own flat." (P03-D)

**Contradicting evidence**

- P01 and P02 book for themselves and never mention proxies.

**Inferred**

- The brief lists "elderly residents" as requesters. In this sample, the older resident isn't the person using the app. Their family member is.
