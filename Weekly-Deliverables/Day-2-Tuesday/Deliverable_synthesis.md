**Loud but shallow: price fairness.**
It sounded like a top theme. P06's line that unconnected older residents "get charged four hundred pounds for a job worth eighty" is the most memorable in the interviews. On audit, only 3 participants raised it, and no one described an overcharge that actually happened to them. P06 heard it secondhand ("I hear about it afterwards from a daughter or a son"), and P03's daughter describes a fear, not an event: "She'd pay it and not tell me."

## Problem statements, ranked

### 1. Requesters struggle to find trusted providers outside their existing network

Requesters with few local connections often have no one to ask when they need someone to work in their home. Because trust in local providers is largely built through personal recommendations, they may have to rely on sources they trust less or wait until someone happens to recommend a provider. This is a problem because requesters may delay getting help or avoid booking altogether, limiting Vello's ability to serve people who do not already have a strong local network.

### 2. Providers struggle to reach customers beyond their existing network

Providers who rely on word of mouth have limited ways to reach customers outside their existing referral network. Their ability to grow depends on how many people already know and recommend them, rather than simply on the quality of their work. This is a problem because good providers can struggle to find new customers, limiting the supply available on Vello and making it harder for the marketplace to grow.

### 3. Requesters are less likely to trust a provider based on platform checks alone

Requesters often place more trust in a recommendation from someone they know than in checks or assurances provided by a company. When there is no personal recommendation behind a provider, requesters may continue looking for help through their existing networks instead of booking through Vello. This is a problem because Vello may struggle to turn new requesters into bookings and repeat users, limiting the value of the app.
