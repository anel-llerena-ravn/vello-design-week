# Prototype Gaps and How the Brief Closed Them

The brief was tested by prototyping its core flow in Claude Design. Every decision the prototype had to make that the brief didn't cover was logged as a gap. Gaps came from clicking through the prototype screen by screen and from the list of decisions Claude Design reported making on its own.

| # | Gap the prototype exposed | How it's closed | Brief section |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Three screens went to the provider's app, which is a separate system, and took focus away from the requester's decision | The provider's side was removed from the core flow, and the provider's flow is listed as out of scope | Define better the Who, Constraints |
| 2 | Only the happy path was designed. No loading, failed or empty states | Every action must cover loading, success and error states | Constraints |
| 3 | One-to-one messaging disappeared, because the brief never said the app already had it | Existing features are listed and must stay, including messaging before booking | Constraints |
| 4 | Newcomers got a different, longer profile than established providers, which made them look like a weaker option | Newly verified providers get the same information structure as everyone else | Constraints |
| 5 | Payment moved off the platform ("You pay Ama directly") | Payment is out of scope, and a completed booking is defined without it | Adjust the Goals, Constraints |
| 6 | Requests went out on a fixed 2-mile radius | Requests reach providers through their service area, with no fixed radius | Constraints |
| 7 | Nothing said who could see a provider's reply | A provider's reply is visible only to that requester | Constraints |
